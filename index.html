About Me

Before I designed interfaces,
I wrote the code that powered them.

I didn’t wake up one day and decide to become a product designer. I actually started my career on the other side of the fence: writing code as a UI developer.

Back then, our team was constantly bumping up against weird product walls. We were writing clean code, but users kept bouncing. The existing website couldn’t clearly explain what the business actually did, design fundamentals were being tossed out the window, and nobody seemed to know why people were dropping off. Instead of just grumbling about confusing specs in our backlog, I got curious: Why build something perfectly if nobody can figure out how to use it?

How I Work

Good design isn't just tidy screens. It's untangling messy systems.

I've spent enough time in Figma to know that moving rectangles around is the easy part. The interesting stuff happens when workflows get messy, requirements keep changing, and a seemingly simple feature turns into twenty different edge cases.

That's the part I enjoy. Figuring out what matters, making sense of the chaos, and turning complicated problems into products that feel surprisingly simple.

Making Complex Things Feel Simple

Give me a dashboard with fifteen filters, confusing navigation, and a workflow nobody wants to touch. I'll happily dig into it.

I enjoy finding the logic beneath the mess and making complicated experiences feel effortless.

Design That Developers Won't Hate

A beautiful design means very little if it falls apart during development.

I like bringing engineering into the conversation early, understanding what's practical, and finding solutions that work beyond Figma.

AI Does the Heavy Lifting. I Do the Thinking.

I'm all for working smarter, especially when AI can take repetitive work off my plate.

From exploring ideas to testing edge cases, I use it to spend less time on busywork and more time solving actual problems.

Components With a Purpose

I don't build design systems just to have a perfectly organised Figma library.

I build reusable components and patterns that help products stay consistent as they grow and make life easier for everyone building them.

Finding the Problems Nobody Asked About

Sometimes the biggest UX problem isn't the screen everyone wants redesigned.

It's the confusing label, the unnecessary step, or that one interaction users keep getting stuck on. I enjoy finding those little things before jumping into solutions.

From Messy Briefs to Meaningful Products

Not every project starts with a clear problem statement and a perfectly written PRD. Most don't.

I enjoy working through the ambiguity, connecting user needs with business goals, and staying involved as ideas turn into real experiences.

My Process

Less Chaos. More Clarity.
Better Products.

There's no magic formula behind my process. Just curiosity, practical thinking, the right tools, and a healthy amount of collaboration. Here's how I bring it all together.

Smarter Workflows

I use AI to speed up repetitive tasks, explore ideas, and catch things I might otherwise miss. That leaves more room for the decisions that actually need a designer.

Better Together

Good ideas get better when different perspectives come together. I involve product and engineering early so we're solving the same problem, not working in separate directions.

People First

Before jumping into solutions, I try to understand what people are struggling with and why. The goal isn't to design more screens, but to make their experience genuinely easier.

Intentional Design

I love beautiful interfaces, but I don't follow trends just because they look good. Every layout, interaction, and visual detail should have a reason to exist.

Real Impact

A polished design is only part of the job. What matters is whether it solves the original problem and what we can learn from how people actually use it.
