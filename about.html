About Me

Before I designed interfaces,
I wrote the code that powered them.

I didn’t wake up one day and decide to become a product designer. I actually started my career on the other side of the fence: writing code as a UI developer.

Back then, our team was constantly bumping up against weird product walls. We were writing clean code, but users kept bouncing. The existing website couldn’t clearly explain what the business actually did, design fundamentals were being tossed out the window, and nobody seemed to know why people were dropping off. Instead of just grumbling about confusing specs in our backlog, I got curious: Why build something perfectly if nobody can figure out how to use it?

I put my hand up to tackle my first redesign right there at my first company. The goal wasn’t to make it look fancy; it was simply to get visitors to understand the product. That first project hooked me on the why behind interfaces.

From there, curiosity turned into craft. In my next role, I left front-end handoff behind and went all-in on design—mastering Figma, running scrappy user interviews, deconstructing competitor products, and building reusable component systems so our teams didn't keep reinventing the wheel.

By the time I stepped into my next role, I was taking complete ownership: taking three distinct software products from a blank whiteboard all the way to production. Having written code myself, I never designed in an ivory tower—I designed things that made sense to real users and could actually be built by the engineers in the room.

“Eight years in, the foundation is still simple: design things that solve real problems, and can actually be built by the team in the room.”
